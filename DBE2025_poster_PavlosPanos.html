Super-Resolution Image Enhancement for 3D Morphological Lung MRI with bSTAR at 0.55T

Panos P.1,2, Pradella M.1, Bieri O.1,2, Hostettler K.E.3, Bauman G.1,2

1Division of Radiological Physics, Department of Radiology, University of Basel Hospital, Basel, Switzerland

2Department of Biomedical Engineering, University of Basel, Basel, Switzerland

3Clinics of Respiratory Medicine, University Hospital Basel, Basel, Switzerland

Introduction

Despite the development of various dedicated techniques for morphological lung imaging, MRI offers limited spatial resolution compared to computed tomography (CT). With the rapid progress in AI-based image enhancement techniques, our study aims to improve lung MRI quality using a deep learning-based super-resolution model, thereby enabling better visualization of lung morphology. In this work, we applied a pre-trained 2D image enhancement model to 3D lung MRI datasets acquired at 0.55 T to enhance image quality.

Purpose

Due to physical and physiological challenges, lung MRI is currently rarely used in clinical routine. Our study demonstrates that a deep learning-based super-resolution image enhancement results in improved visualization of MRI lung morphology without introducing new anatomical structures.

Methods

Data Acquisition

MRI data was acquired on a 0.55T MAGNETOM Free.Max scanner. We used two structural, free-breathing lung MRI sequences that both provided isotropic resolution:

  • 3D half-radial bSTAR: 1.3 mm isotropic resolution.
  • 3D stack-of-spirals UTE: 2.0 mm isotropic resolution.

Super-Resolution Model

We used a pre-trained 2D super-resolution model based on the Dense Residual Channel Attention (DRCT) architecture to perform a 4× upscale. Notably, the model was trained on large-scale natural image datasets (DF2K and OST300), not on prior medical images.

3D Enhancement Workflow

Our memory-efficient workflow enhances the 3D volume by applying the 2D model across all three spatial dimensions. This approach ensures uniform sharpness and minimizes artifacts.

Discussion and Conclusion

  • Achieves 4× resolution increase without heavy memory use.
  • No artificial structures added — critical for clinical trust.
  • Sequence-agnostic, not pathology-tuned → minimizes model bias.
